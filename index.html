The generation that drinks differently

U.S. drinking just hit its lowest level in the ~90 years Gallup has tracked it, and young adults are leading the retreat — but the full picture is more useful than the headline. Gen Z hasn't quit drinking; it has become selective, occasional, and format-driven: fewer occasions, fewer categories per occasion, and a fast rotation into RTDs, no-alcohol beer, THC drinks, and functional alternatives. This tracker compiles the sourced numbers behind that shift so the moves show up here before they show up in your sales reports.

01

The big picture: less drinking, chosen deliberately

Two datasets tell the core story. Gallup's U.S. survey shows participation falling off a cliff among young adults, and IWSR's global volume data shows 2025 shrinking almost everywhere — with ready-to-drink formats and no-alcohol beer the only green on the board.

READ OF THE DATA

This is a volume problem, not just a youth problem. Every generation is moderating — IWSR found 75% of Gen Z no/low drinkers actively moderating, but also 74% of Millennials and 66% of Gen X. The money isn't disappearing; it's concentrating into fewer, more deliberate occasions — and the first (sometimes only) drink of the night is where the fight now happens.

Brand watch: where the cans are moving

Real brands, real numbers. Spirits-based RTDs ate the malt-seltzer boom; no-alcohol beer is compounding at 20%+; and a billion-dollar THC drinks category is racing a federal deadline. Filter by lane. Read every brand figure as a total-market number — no published Gen-Z-only brand sales splits exist, so none are shown or implied here.

High Noon: the RTD rocket flattens

Million nine-litre cases per year, U.S. — Gallo's vodka seltzer nearly tripled in three years, then fell ~3% in 2025, its first decline. It remains the #1 spirits brand by volume in the U.S.; the category leader plateauing is itself a signal.

03

The counter-narrative: Gen Z is coming of age, not opting out

Before betting the company on abstinence, weigh IWSR's Bevtrac panel (32,000+ respondents, 15 markets). As more of Gen Z reaches legal drinking age, its participation has been climbing toward the adult norm — while the shape of each occasion shrinks. Both things are true at once, and the strategy differs depending on which one you weight.

SO WHAT FOR A BEV COMPANY

Plan for fewer, richer occasions: one or two drinks, chosen on flavor and format, often a cocktail or RTD, frequently alternated with something non-alcoholic. The losing position is undifferentiated volume — commodity beer and wine by the case. The winning positions in the data are premium RTDs, credible NA versions of flagship brands, and cocktail culture, where 84% of Gen Z drinkers already participate.

04

Signals from the social floor

The behaviors bubbling up on TikTok, Reddit, and in bars — most pinned to a survey or sales dataset, with a strength read on how hard it's biting. The purely cultural ones are labeled qualitative, not dressed up as statistics. This is the early-warning layer: these are the patterns that show up in feeds and forums months before they show up in scanner data.

47%

Zebra striping is normal now

Nearly half of U.S. on-premise visitors already alternate alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks within a single night out — moderation built into the occasion rather than announced. In the UK it's ~35% of 18–24 pub-goers.

SIGNAL
Source: NielsenIQ on-premise research, early 2025 · analysis
49%

Half the country plans to drink less

49% of Americans planned to drink less in 2025 — a 44% jump in that intention vs. 2023. Intent doesn't always become behavior, but intent at this scale is what fills NA shelf sets and mocktail menus.

SIGNAL
Source: NCSolutions consumer survey · Food Institute coverage
36%

A third of Gen Z has never started

36% of Gen Z consumers of legal drinking age have never consumed alcohol at all, and just 17% of drinkers drink weekly (down from 23% in 2020). The on-ramp itself is narrowing — first-drink brands matter more than ever.

SIGNAL
Source: Euromonitor International, 2025 · coverage
66%

Health belief flipped in one generation

Two-thirds of adults 18–34 now say even moderate drinking (1–2 drinks a day) is bad for your health — vs. 50% of 35–54 and 48% of 55+. This belief, turbocharged by the Surgeon General's cancer-risk advisory, is the engine under every other signal here.

SIGNAL
Source: Gallup, Jul 2025 · coverage
28%

Dry January fatigue — moderation goes quiet

Gen Z participation in monthlong abstinence slipped from 30% to 28% across 15 markets (UK: 33% → 24%). Performative all-or-nothing sobriety is fading; the durable behavior is low-key, year-round moderation — zebra striping, not pledges.

SIGNAL
Source: IWSR Bevtrac, Autumn 2025 · IWSR
4%

Gen Z is only 4% of sales — for now

Gen Z (21+) accounted for just 4% of U.S. alcohol sales in 2025 vs. 25% Millennial and 70% Gen X + Boomer (Circana). Their current wallet is small; their influence on formats, flavors, and what's stocked is not. Watch the cohort age into spending power.

SIGNAL
Source: Circana, 52 weeks to Dec 28, 2025 · The Daily Pour
1B+

#mocktails is a media category now

The #mocktails hashtag has passed a billion views on TikTok, and "hangxiety" — next-day anxiety after drinking — is now standard vocabulary in younger drinkers' feeds. This is cultural salience, not a sales statistic — but salience is what stocks menus and shelf sets a season later.

SIGNAL
Qualitative signal · TikTok hashtag volume as reported in industry coverage, 2025–26
$18–20

What the forums keep saying

Recurring themes across Reddit and drinking forums: bar cocktails at $18–20 push drinking home or into pre-gaming for economy, and hangover and sleep quality dominate the "why I cut back" threads. Themes, not statistics — but they rhyme with what the surveys measure.

05

Why it's happening: the drivers, ranked by evidence

Ranked by strength of evidence — not by how often each gets repeated in pitch decks. The top of this list is measured in national surveys; the bottom is real but easy to overstate, and it's labeled that way.

1

Health beliefs flipped

53% of U.S. adults — and about two-thirds of 18–34s — now say one or two drinks a day is bad for your health (Gallup, 2025), amplified by the Surgeon General's cancer-risk advisory. This is the engine: nearly every other trend on this page moves with it.

STRONG EVIDENCE
2

Cost and life stage

Bar cocktails at $18–20, alcohol budgets shrinking even among high earners (IWSR Bevtrac, H1 2025), and a generation reaching milestones later on tighter money. When budgets pinch, the casual extra round is the first thing cut — and single-serve formats beat the $15 multipack.

STRONG EVIDENCE
3

Phones and thinner social calendars

Social life moved on-screen, so there are simply fewer default in-person occasions where drinking happens automatically. BCG's late-2025 consumer survey found drinking shifting away from big gatherings toward quiet moments alone or with one other person.

MODERATE EVIDENCE
4

Moderation culture — and alternatives worth drinking

Zebra striping (47% of on-premise visitors), a genuinely good NA shelf, and mocktail culture turned moderation into the path of least resistance. Crucially, the alternatives finally taste like a choice rather than a punishment.

MODERATE EVIDENCE
5

Cannabis substitution — real, but bounded

Substitution is real in subgroups and specific occasions, and THC drinks are a live category (see Brand Watch). But at population level the evidence is weak: Gallup notes U.S. marijuana use has been roughly flat for four years while drinking fell — substitution alone can't explain the drop.

REAL, OVERSTATED
6

GLP-1 weight-loss drugs

Early clinical evidence suggests GLP-1 medications can reduce alcohol intake, and Gen Z talks about them more openly than any other generation. But this is not yet a proven population-level driver for young drinkers. Watch the research — don't build the plan on it.

EARLY EVIDENCE ONLY

Watchlist: dates that move this market

Upcoming checkpoints where the next trend read will come from — regulation first, then the recurring data drops.

NOV 12, 2026 · DAYS AWAY

Federal hemp-THC ban takes effect

The Nov 2025 federal spending law redefined hemp with a 0.4mg total-THC-per-container cap that effectively bans most intoxicating hemp drinks when it takes effect Nov 12, 2026. A $1B+ category and 500–750 brands face a cliff — or a state-by-state patchwork. Expect consolidation, reformulation to compliant doses, and alcohol companies circling distressed THC brands.

Source: Legal500 industry analysis · link
JANUARY 2027

Dry January 2027 participation read

IWSR Bevtrac's next abstinence wave will show whether Gen Z's monthlong-abstinence slide (30% → 28%) continues. A further drop confirms moderation has gone informal; a rebound would suggest the sober-curious moment has a second act.

Source: IWSR Bevtrac tracking · link
MID-2027

IWSR's next global data release

The June-cycle release will confirm whether global volume fell for a fourth straight year and how 2026 landed for RTDs vs. wine. IWSR's standing forecast: volumes keep declining until growth resumes around 2031, ending 2035 about 1% below 2025 — with U.S. servings down a forecast 18% over the decade.

Source: IWSR forecast release, Jun 2026 · link
ONGOING · THROUGH 2033

NA beer share of the beer market

No-alcohol beer was the second-largest beer subcategory by volume in 2025 at ~2% of global beer. IWSR expects 4% by 2033 and sees no/low adding ~$4B in value by 2028; its wider U.S. no-alc universe reaches ~$5B by 2028 — a different measure from NielsenIQ's ~$1B scan total, and the two are kept separate on this page on purpose.

Sources: IWSR; NielsenIQ via Forbes · link

CLAIMS TO BE CAREFUL WITH

"Gen Z drinks 20% less than Millennials." A 2018-vintage figure still circulating in decks and articles. It is not a current measurement and shouldn't anchor a 2027 plan.

"Half of Gen Z doesn't drink." This conflates Gallup's 18–34 bracket (50% of U.S. 18–34s say they drink) with the whole of Gen Z. IWSR's legal-age Gen Z panel across 15 markets shows 74% participation. Different brackets, different questions, different answers — all three stated on this page with their labels attached.

Generic hard-seltzer growth forecasts. Several market-report mills project healthy seltzer growth while IWSR's measured global seltzer volume fell 10% across 2021–24. When a forecast contradicts measured volume, trust the measurement.
